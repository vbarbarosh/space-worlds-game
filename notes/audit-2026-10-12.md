# World Explorer: performance audit, 2026-10-12

Repository: `vbarbarosh/space-worlds-game`, `/app`, branch `main`, source HEAD `e99975d9b9d70c8bd735f14edc9746cc96bb6335`. Audit begun 12 October and completed 13 October 2026 on the same unchanged source/build. Audit of the current game; no gameplay or rendering fixes applied by this audit.

## Scope

This audit covers simulation, collision and targeting complexity, effects, 2D and 3D rendering, caches, loading, resize, HUD and menus, campaign saving, audio scheduling, and the performance harness. It reassesses the findings of 10 October. The requested deliverable is this evidence-backed Markdown register, with critical paths and an order of work.

The player's concern is responsiveness during fights and after a long session. The implementation review follows allocations, object lifetimes, repeated scans, synchronous work and render passes. Large saved campaigns and repeated world transitions are examined as workload boundaries; this is not a security audit.

Nothing here establishes mobile thermals, battery use, real GPU/VRAM consumption, Firefox/Safari behavior, or multi-hour stability. Large campaign-building counts are a source-based scaling analysis unless explicitly measured below. No claim that every possible configuration is fast follows from this audit.

## Verdict

The most serious confirmed defect is **linear retention of 3D stations on world changes**: ten calls through the current world-change path leave ten station groups and 850 meshes in the scene. This is now relevant to the ordinary game's 3D setting, not just a developer prototype. The prior report's blanket “no leak” conclusion cannot cover this path.

The current game also does avoidable synchronous save/menu work and repeated transient allocation. Crowded fights require substantially more rendering and allocation than calm flight. Collision and campaign-building scans have clear scaling limits, but a complexity formula alone does not prove that they are today's largest bottleneck.

## Findings

### WE-25 3D world changes retain old stations (high)

`src/js/render3d/scene.js:122` clears only `render3d.models`. At line 136 it assigns a new station and adds it to `render3d.scene`, without removing the old station. Stations are not entries in the models map. Repeated visits therefore retain reachable groups, meshes, and each station's private beacon material. Old stations also remain eligible for traversal, rendering and shadow work; actual draw contribution depends on the camera and their previous positions.

Reproduction: `node data/perf/audit-20261012/station-lifetime.cjs`. It loads the installed Three.js and the unmodified model and world-set functions into a VM, alternates two world definitions, and counts scene children and meshes. This isolates scene ownership from GPU performance.

| World sets | Station groups | Meshes | Shared geometries | Shared materials |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 | 85 | 12 | 5 |
| 2 | 2 | 170 | 12 | 7 |
| 5 | 5 | 425 | 12 | 7 |
| 10 | 10 | 850 | 12 | 7 |

The constant shared-cache counts show that the growing station instances, not new geometry variants, cause this result. This is a deterministic retention defect, not merely an increasing-heap hypothesis. Remove or reuse the previous station before replacement. Dispose its uniquely owned materials while keeping shared geometry/material caches valid. Verify repeated world trips, new runs, arcade progression, and switching between 2D and 3D. Do not infer an exact MB or FPS penalty from this mesh count.

### WE-26 3D prepares the whole world and draws individual meshes (medium)

`src/js/render3d/scene.js:263` loops over every ore node and enemy, creates models for unseen objects, and updates transforms whether they are in the camera or not. `src/js/render3d/models.js:49` builds separate `Mesh` objects; the station alone has 85. Three.js can frustum-cull individual renderables, but that does not remove the game's synchronization, object creation, scene traversal, or all shadow work. The 2D path checks visibility before most sprite drawing (`src/js/render.js:89`, `src/js/render3d/scene.js:399`).

This creates an initial world-entry cost and a CPU floor that depends on the whole world rather than only the visible scene. Shared geometries/materials are a useful existing optimization. Candidate work: profile object setup separately, lazily create models near the view with a shadow margin, and consider instancing repeated rocks or merging static station parts. Preserve offscreen simulation and shadows that affect visible objects. No broad-phase rewrite is justified solely by mesh count.

### WE-27 Model retirement has no owned-material disposal (low, lifecycle risk)

`src/js/render3d/models.js:17` creates private materials for animated lights; the player owns two (`:88`) and each station owns a beacon. Removal in `src/js/render3d/scene.js:132` and `:295` only detaches groups and deletes map entries. No `dispose()` path exists in these game files.

Removing scene objects and disposing renderer resources are separate operations. Once the station retention is fixed, establish explicit ownership for private materials and dispose them on model retirement. Shared geometries and shared materials must remain alive while another model uses them. This review did not measure persistent GPU resource growth after player replacement, so this is an ownership gap rather than a quantified VRAM leak. Clearing all shared caches on every transition would add rebuild stalls and is not the recommended fix.

### WE-28 Checkpoint saves rebuild hidden art and serialize synchronously (medium)

`src/js/checkpoint.js:1` stores world state, deep-copies the campaign using JSON stringify/parse (`:32`), stringifies the checkpoint again, writes localStorage, then calls `menu_continue_show` (`:46–48`). That calls `menu_art_sync` (`src/js/menu.js:158–168`). At `src/js/menu.js:182`, every save re-serializes the ship SVG, encodes planet/station artwork and resets image URLs for the menu's world cards, even during flight with the menu hidden.

Saves are not just a 15-second timer: drone losses, rock destruction in Dustfall, cargo delivery, structures, contracts and purchases also call the same function. Examples: `src/js/physics.js:7`, `src/js/drones.js:212`, `src/js/structures.js:260`. Several events in one update can perform repeated full snapshots. A larger campaign increases serialization work. Browser caches may reuse image decoding; the JavaScript encoding and DOM assignments still execute.

Targeted reproduction: 30 consecutive saves of the small benchmark campaign invoked `menu_art_sync` **30 times**. Direct synchronous call time: median 4.80 ms, maximum 9.80 ms; stored snapshot 41,704 characters. This microbenchmark demonstrates redundant work; it is not the normal autosave frequency or a mature-campaign latency guarantee. Raw data: `save.json`.

`src/js/saves.js:37` also reparses the complete six-slot JSON several times while refreshing the Load/Last save controls (`saves_read`, `last_save_slot`, then `saves_read` again when a slot exists). This is a bounded, lower-frequency instance of repeated serialization work; read the slots once per refresh.

Cache menu artwork by world/ship and update it when the relevant visible menu changes. Coalesce same-frame saves without losing the final state, and retain explicit immediate-save behavior at departure/close boundaries. Separate simulation snapshots from hidden-menu rendering. Any storage change needs failure/quota and restore checks, not just a faster average time.

### WE-29 Every active missile blast copies all projectiles (medium)

`src/js/missiles.js:309`, inside the wave loop:

```js
for (const b of [...bullets, ...hostile]) {
```

Each damaging wave below chain depth three copies both complete arrays before discarding non-missile shots. With W waves and B+H projectiles this adds O(W*(B+H)) copied references and scans. The list caps at 40 waves; it is not an infinite chain.

A deterministic VM count probe against the current missile function with 40 eligible waves and 300 non-missile projectiles counted **12,000 iterator visits and 40 combined arrays in one update**. This is a synthetic ceiling case, not an observed typical battle. Traverse the two existing lists directly while preserving order and the processing of newly appended waves. Profile before adding a separate missile index. Visible missile-trail rendering also copies trail points (`src/js/missiles.js:392`) and sometimes filters them again (`:408`); include that in allocation profiling.

### WE-30 Campaign structures and nearest-target scans scale with accumulated play (medium risk)

`src/js/structures.js:38` filters the entire campaign structure list for the current world. This occurs from updates, collision construction and individual raider target lookups. There is no explicit build-count cap (`:116–148`); resources, spacing and world area constrain it instead.

Each current-world outpost scans ore (`:243`, `:268`); platforms filter and sort nearby enemies to take the nearest (`:335`), even while their weapons cool down. Transport mounts repeat nearest-only sorts (`src/js/transports.js:237`). Each structure checks hostile shots and enemies (`src/js/structures.js:352`). Route planning repeatedly sorts remaining outposts (`src/js/transports.js:67`), although retries are throttled.

For total structures T, local outposts O, platforms P, rocks R, enemies E, hostile shots H and structure-targeting raiders A, this adds roughly O(A*T + O*R + P*E log E + (O+P)*(H+E)) per frame, plus hull collision costs. Sorts apply to the nearby candidate subset. This is definite work, not a measured mature-campaign slowdown.

Add 10/50/100-building saves to the benchmark suite. Begin with a linear nearest search, membership caching with correct invalidation, and less frequent decisions where targets have not changed. Measure before introducing a spatial index.

### WE-31 The performance harness cannot certify smooth gameplay (medium, tooling)

`bin/perf:88` prints page errors without making the run fail. `bin/perf:182` reports timing distributions and long-frame counts, but `main()` imposes no performance budget or regression threshold. Successful completion means measurements were written, not that frame time passed a limit. This differs from the blanket exit-on-failure expectation in AGENTS.md.

The 38 scenes are 2D, newly loaded pages with little persistent campaign history. The harness calls private abilities directly, has no audio gesture, and forces virtual time. Its default 240+60+300 frames are only ten seconds, too short to cover periodic 15-second autosaves. It misses world-return cache growth, 3D transitions, most screens, hangar previews, sustained actual input and high-refresh emission. Shorter broad sweeps miss even more rare behavior; targeted tests are required.

Make browser errors fatal, preserve raw evidence, and define separate budgets for simulation, render submission, layout and wall-clock responsiveness on specified hardware. Add representative persistent-state scenarios. A shared-headless-machine FPS threshold alone would produce noisy failures.

### WE-32 Pixel area and composite passes need an explicit quality budget (medium)

`src/js/helpers.js:88` sizes the game canvas to window dimensions times a DPR capped at 1.7. At 1920×1080 this is 3264×1836, about 6.0 million pixels, versus 2.1 million at DPR 1. Full-screen background drawing, transparent glows and overlap repeatedly touch these pixels.

The 3D path adds a similarly sized antialiased WebGL target, renders a 2048×2048 shadow map, then copies the WebGL canvas into the 2D canvas (`src/js/render3d/scene.js:92`, `:341–356`). Reduced effects removes some glows and particles, but does not lower render resolution or disable the 3D shadow map. `shadows=0` is a URL option rather than a normal quality setting.

This is a known cost multiplier, not proof that any particular GPU cannot handle it. Consider a user quality control with independently measured resolution, shadow and effect settings; retain a readable DOM HUD. Profile background and blended passes separately. Avoid automatically changing gameplay or reducing collision accuracy to improve drawing speed.

### WE-33 Unchanged HUD classes still cause geometry work (low)

The game throttles `update_hud()` to about 8 Hz (`src/js/boot.js:32`) and guards many text/style writes. However, `src/js/hud.js:188` and `:258` assign a complete class string each update. `src/js/hud_layout.js:15` observes class/hidden attributes across the HUD; its callback queries all safe rectangles and calls `getBoundingClientRect()` (`:19–32`). Assigning an unchanged class can still enqueue an attribute mutation.

With the game held still, 30 repeated HUD updates produced **30 class/hidden attribute mutation records** after the first update had been drained. The observer batches records; this is not a claim of 30 separate layout passes. Raw targets and counts: `save.json`.

Guard identical class writes and coalesce geometry invalidations once per frame. Preserve updates on actual resize, visibility and layout changes. A mutation count proves redundant work, not by itself a large layout stall; prioritize below measured rendering and lifetime defects.

### WE-34 Sprite raster variants remain across worlds and zooms (medium)

`src/js/sprites.js:4` keeps the sprite cache for the page lifetime. Each sprite's nested maps retain every colour, layer and raster-size variant (`:101–137`), with no byte budget or eviction. `sprite_draw_box` uses quantized powers of two, a 32-pixel minimum and a maximum of 1024 or twice the SVG box (`:177`), so a fixed set of assets/colours has a finite number of size variants. This is cache accumulation, not an automatically unbounded leak.

A rendered tour of all eight worlds at zoom 50%, 100% and 200%, repeated twice, ended at **197 cached canvases**, representing **144.54 MiB** of nominal RGBA pixels. First-tour end: 197 canvases / 144.54 MiB. This excludes other canvases, browser overhead, GPU copies and decoded SVG resources. A short three-frame stop may create further variants on a later pass, so two tours do not prove a final upper bound. Raw data: `cache.json`.

Both measured tours ended at the same cache size; this tested route plateaued. Track raster bytes as well as JS heap: canvas backing storage is not fully represented by heap-used size. If the measured retained footprint is problematic on target devices, use a byte-bounded cache or retire cold world-specific rasters. Test returning to old worlds to balance memory against repeated SVG decoding and raster hitches.

### WE-35 Trail emission scales with display refresh (low)

`src/js/update.js:258–272` creates a trail point per moving frame and attempts an exhaust particle with probability 0.7 per frame. Lifetimes bound the live list, but a 144-Hz display can create 2.4 times as many objects per wall second as 60 Hz, with denser trails and more gravity/filter work. Other engine emitters already use elapsed time (`src/js/engine.js:14`). Use time/distance-based emission with bounded catch-up if stable appearance and allocation rates are desired. This is source arithmetic; a 144-Hz display was not tested.

### WE-36 Slow frames also slow the simulation (informational)

`src/js/boot.js:13` limits one update to 0.033 seconds and does one update per rendered frame. At a sustained 20 FPS, one wall second advances at most 0.66 simulation seconds; at 10 FPS, 0.33. Long pauses discard simulated time. This protects collision stability and avoids a catch-up spiral, but responsiveness and gameplay speed are coupled.

Treat this as an explicit overload policy. If real-time progression is required, consider bounded fixed-step simulation and interpolation, with collision and weapon correctness checks. Removing the cap alone would create large physics jumps. Virtual-clock benchmark seconds must not be reported as real-time FPS.

### WE-37 Music set to zero still schedules voices (low)

The music switch changes `volume_settings.music` (`src/js/settings.js:17–26`). `schedule_music()` decides activity from game state, visibility and global mute (`src/js/music.js:80`), not music volume. It still calls `music_step()` while the music gain is zero. Oscillators and envelopes are therefore still created for inaudible music; the overall sound mute takes a different path.

After an audio-start gesture, the context was running. In two successive four-second observations, music volume 0.65 made 2 tone calls; volume zero still made **12 tone calls**, with 10 voices live at the second sample. This confirms synthesis continues while music is inaudible; audio hardware power/latency was not measured. Raw data: `audio.json`.

Skip inaudible music scheduling while keeping the intended restart position and fade behavior. Audio cleanup is otherwise explicit: stopped voices disconnect on completion, old world buses retire after nine seconds, and the scheduler limits its look-ahead rather than endlessly catching up.

### WE-38 The 2D page carries all 3D and sprite bytes (low)

`src/index.html:8–19` embeds Three.js inside `three_from_package()`; the library executes only on enabling 3D, but its source is still delivered in the initial HTML. `bin/build:96` embeds every SVG. The current built game is **5,486,350 bytes**, locally gzipped to **1,252,012 bytes**; dev is 5,533,920 bytes and agent 5,497,178 bytes. These are artifact sizes, not measured network transfer sizes or server compression claims.

The embedded Three.js source alone is 2,093,053 bytes (411,754 locally gzipped); the source tree contains 311 SVGs totaling 1,918,937 bytes before packing.

The single-file offline packaging is intentional and has value. Treat splitting/lazy loading as a packaging decision with offline consequences, not an automatic improvement. Compression and removing demonstrably unused packed assets are lower-risk candidates. Startup timing is reported above; no mobile/network loading claim is made.

### WE-39 Hangar previews redraw every card at full device DPR (low)

`src/js/hangar_preview.js:20` queries and draws every preview while the hangar tab is open; no intersection/scroll visibility test exists. `:73` uses raw `devicePixelRatio`, unlike the game's 1.7 cap. Every card rebuilds anchor arrays, finds the longest ship and maps its loadout each frame (`:79–97`). Six current ship cards bound the workload, but offscreen cards still consume CPU/raster work and DPR 3 makes nine times the pixel area of DPR 1.

Cache invariant anchors/ship scale per preview, reuse the existing WeakMap, and skip cards outside the scroll viewport. Cap preview DPR independently if target measurements justify it. The WeakMap and finite shot TTL are good lifecycle choices. This report does not claim a preview leak.

The hangar probe counted **126 preview draws** during its requested 1.2-second observation with six ship cards. This verifies repeated preview work, not its cost on a physical GPU.

### WE-40 Collision and target scans multiply object counts (low at current scale)

`src/js/bodies.js:80` runs four settling passes. At `:145`, each pass tests every enemy against every static body and then every enemy pair: 4*E*S static candidates and 4*E*(E−1)/2 pair candidates per frame, plus player checks. Avoidance adds approximately E*S more candidates (`:51`). Typed arrays and squared-distance/axis rejections substantially reduce the cost per candidate.

Player shots scan enemies, rocks and drifting debris (`src/js/update.js:421` onward); hostile shots scan solid ore (`src/js/flight.js`, `block_hostile_ore`); missile fuse/lock paths scan enemies (`src/js/missiles.js:153`, `:250`). Gravity visits live object lists against each black hole (`src/js/map.js:230`). Evasive thrusters build a fresh obstacle list while active (`src/js/evasion.js:44`). World-label placement can make eight passes over previous rectangles and sort four escape directions on overlaps (`src/js/world_labels.js:74`), so crowded labels also add superlinear work.

These paths are critical to future scale, but most broad-sweep update means are small relative to rendering in this environment. Ordinary spawn gates are below 24 enemies in a campaign, 12 for patrols, and 14 before an arcade squadron; these gates can be exceeded by a squadron or splitter children. A shared spatial candidate index may eventually help multiple consumers, but changing only one scan would leave the others. Verify broad-phase candidates against the same collision, targeting and world-placement rules; do not skip offscreen physics.

### WE-20 Duplicate filtering remains (low, prior finding)

The current update removes dead particles, trail, bullets, hostile shots, enemies and pickups (`src/js/update.js:76`, `:89`, `:559–579`); gravity filters those lists again (`src/js/map.js:263–268`). Hull separation additionally filters enemies (`src/js/bodies.js:82`) and gravity copies them with `slice()` (`src/js/map.js:232`). This creates arrays and repeats work each frame.

Consolidating passes needs an ordering review: callers can depend on when a dead object disappears or on an old array reference. Start with profiles and explicit lifetime phases, not indiscriminate in-place mutation.

### WE-21 Allocation and GC remain an investigation priority (medium, prior finding)

Across the 38 short traces, allocation estimates ranged 221–838 KiB/frame. There were 27 major events in total, with per-scene counts 0–3; the longest observed GC event was 44.80 ms. Do not compare these counts directly to the old 300-frame report: this sweep measures 120 frames and uses a newer browser/build. The longest callback was 173.60 ms, so GC cannot simply be declared the sole explanation for all slow callbacks. CPU/allocation sampling is summarized with the measurements above.

Particles, shots, trails and nested explosions are created and discarded; they have many lifetime/cap safeguards but are not pooled throughout. Sampling can identify allocation-heavy functions; it cannot alone prove which retained object caused a major collection. The old audit's explanation that promoted short-lived effects cause every hitch should be treated as a hypothesis, not a demonstrated universal cause. Remove redundant temporary arrays first; introduce bounded pools only where profiles and repeat measurements justify their complexity.

### WE-22 The arcade harness can accumulate effects on a stopped screen (medium, tooling; prior finding)

`bin/perf`'s scene driver directly calls pulse/EMP functions periodically without checking `state === 'playing'`. A cleared screen can stop effect aging while the harness continues to create waves. The player's ordinary pulse action checks the playing state (`src/js/combat.js:360`). Thus a long arcade harness run can manufacture growth that does not represent player behavior. The source path remains; no such growth is labelled a gameplay leak in this report. Gate benchmark actions by the game state and report time actually spent fighting.

### WE-23 Resize remains a large-canvas operation (low, prior finding)

`src/js/helpers.js:93–94` reallocates the backing canvas on each resize event, even before considering the cached background and 3D renderer's subsequent size change. There is no coalescing of a stream of resize events. The 3D path resizes its own render target on the next draw. Large software raster work can stall the page.

After a campaign start, resizing 1366×768 to 2560×1440 at DPR 2 produced 1 observed long tasks in the next 1.5 seconds, longest **1027 ms**. These entries cover the entire resize observation, not exclusively the `resize()` function. This was one software-rendered resize; it does not establish the distribution during interactive dragging. Raw data: `startup.json`.

Coalesce resize requests to one per animation frame and avoid assignments when dimensions have not changed if target profiling shows this matters. Do not transfer the previous audit's 0.6-second estimate to new devices without measurement.

### WE-24 3D is now a regular game option (medium, prior finding updated)

`src/js/settings.js:33` exposes the view toggle in the game, and `src/js/render3d/scene.js:452` stores the choice. The old “dev mode only” qualification is obsolete. The path adds model synchronization, matrices, lighting, shadow drawing and full-canvas compositing. It keeps a fixed pool of blast lights to avoid changing shader light counts, which is a useful safeguard.

A short sequential software-backend diagnostic (one calm Haven scene, 1366×768, DPR 2) measured:

- 2d: median callback 4.60 ms over 24 measured frames after six warm-up draws; 3D active=False, scene meshes=0, models=0.
- 3d: median callback 471.10 ms over 24 measured frames after six warm-up draws; 3D active=True, scene meshes=316, models=108.
- 3d-no-shadows: median callback 340.30 ms over 24 measured frames after six warm-up draws; 3D active=True, scene meshes=316, models=108.

These variants occur at successive simulation times and are not a paired GPU benchmark. Scene and renderer counters, visible versus total ore, and individual callback times are in `3d.json`. No recommendation to disable shadows is based solely on this software run.

## What is good

- The WE-16–19 fixes remain: coordinates are read once in the gravity/debris loops, colour prefixes use a bounded cache, and sprite raster keys use nested maps.
- 2D scenery, particles, labels and many objects are culled; projectiles are batched in render-cache passes; background and glow assets are cached.
- Hull separation uses typed arrays and broad-phase rejects. Ordinary spawns are throttled; they are admission thresholds rather than an absolute global enemy cap.
- Major effect lists have caps or TTLs: explosions 140, wrecks 10, missile waves 40, chain depth three, pulse sparks 400. `burst` trims particles to 600, although other emitters can temporarily exceed that number.
- Modal views skip simulation and repeated scene rendering; background-tab handling can pause play. The main loop schedules one next animation frame rather than an expanding task queue.
- HUD writes are largely throttled/guarded, toast count is capped, previews use a WeakMap, audio nodes have completion cleanup, and old music buses are retired.
- Game randomness and effect randomness are separated in important effects. Any optimization should preserve gameplay decisions and the deterministic benchmark scenario.

## Recommended order of work

1. Fix WE-25 station ownership first; add a repeated-world-transition scene whose station/mesh counts stay bounded. Include private-material retirement from WE-27.
2. Remove redundant checkpoint/menu work (WE-28), then per-wave projectile copies (WE-29), and verify both behavior and allocation changes.
3. Make benchmark errors meaningful and expand persistent/3D/UI/audio coverage (WE-22, WE-31). Establish target-device baselines before asserting an FPS budget.
4. Profile 3D synchronization/draw calls and high-pixel-area passes (WE-24, WE-26, WE-32). Expose useful quality controls if measured benefits justify them.
5. Reassess GC with repeat allocation profiles (WE-20, WE-21). Pool only proven hot lifetimes, preserving effect order and caps.
6. Test mature campaign structures (WE-30), then reduce nearest-only sorts and repeated world membership queries. Collision indexing is conditional on measured scale.
7. Address guarded HUD writes, raster cache budgets, refresh-independent emission, inaudible music and offscreen previews (WE-33–35, WE-37, WE-39). Keep offline packaging and low-FPS simulation behavior explicit product decisions (WE-36, WE-38).
