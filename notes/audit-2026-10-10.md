# World Explorer: performance audit, 2026-10-10

Repository: /app, branch main, HEAD 7878b6a (2026-10-10 23:07), measured in a copy; the fixes are patches against that commit in `commondir/perf/` of the note, not applied.

## Scope

Whether the game lags, and why: the frame's time, the garbage it makes, the long frames, memory over long play, start-up, resize, and the 3D dev mode.

Run:
- `bin/perf` (frames mode, ×2, 1920 × 1080) over all 38 scenes: every world calm and in a fight at zoom 100% and 50%, the arcade's worlds 6–8 (`data/perf/base1`).
- `bin/perf --mode=profile` (CPU and allocation sampling) on the three heaviest kinds: fight-w4-z50, calm-w6-z50, arcade-w6-z50, before and after the fixes.
- Before and after the fixes, back to back and twice, on fight-w0-z100, fight-w4-z50, calm-w6-z50, arcade-w6-z50 (`cmp-base-1/2`, `cmp-fix-1/2`).
- `bin/perf --mode=shots` then `--compare` on six scenes, before and after: canvases pixel by pixel, the HUD's text line by line.
- Four minutes of game time per scene (fight-w0-z100, calm-w6-z50, arcade-w6-z50), the heap after a forced collection every 30 s, the DOM nodes, listeners and every per-frame list.
- Start-up and resize of `build/index.html` at ×2 (long tasks), three rounds; a CPU profile of a resize to 2560 × 1440.
- `build/dev.html?world=0&screen=combat` in 2D, 3D and 3D without shadows, on SwiftShader (Chromium's GPU path on the CPU), the readout sampled once a second for 20 s, two rounds.

Not run: any graphics card (this box has none, so every canvas is rastered in software on the main thread, and frame totals rank costs rather than predict them on a GPU); the station, map, menu and other screens beyond their start-up; the agent page; audio; `bin/perf --gpu` over all scenes (too slow here); a real player's input.

Noise: another agent loaded the 4-core box all night (load average 3–6). Frame times ran 2–3 times those of the 5 October report on the same scenes and wandered by 50–100% between runs of the same build; only the allocation per frame and the number of garbage collections held steady (within 2% between runs) and are what this audit compares.

## Verdict

The game's own script is in good shape: the slow part of a frame is the canvas raster and the garbage collector, not the game's logic. Fights make 0.55–0.83 MB of garbage a frame, which means 40–70 minor collections and 1–4 major ones every 5 seconds; the majors are the hitches a player feels in a crowd. Four small fixes cut the garbage by 4–35% with the picture and the simulation unchanged; the larger cut, pooling the short-lived effects, is left as a recommendation.

## Findings

### WE-16 debris_sanctuary boxes a number for every distance (medium)

`src/js/expedition.js:380`. Every drifting piece asks every frame whether it has strayed into the station, a portal or a gate, through `distance()` about ten times; each result came back from a call as a boxed number. Allocation profile, 300 frames: `debris_sanctuary` 18 111 KB in fight-w4-z50, 14 926 KB in calm-w6-z50, 9 958 KB in arcade-w6-z50, that is 60, 50 and 33 KB a frame. Patch 0004 works the same sum inline on a position read once: after it the function is out of the 30 largest allocators.

### WE-17 the gravity pass reads every object's position once per black hole (medium)

`src/js/map.js:230` calls `gravity_move` (`src/js/gravity.js:26`) and `in_gravity_core` (`src/js/render_cache.js:145`) for every enemy, shot, pickup, particle, trail point and rock, every frame; every world has four or more black holes. Each loop read `v.x` and `v.y` once per hole, and a field read across so many object shapes boxes a number. Before: `gravity_move` 53 KB and `in_gravity_core` 52 KB a frame in fight-w4-z50, 50 and 49 KB in calm-w6-z50. Patch 0003 reads the position once and writes it back only when a hole moved it: after, 9 and 8 KB, and 6 and 7 KB.

### WE-18 color_with_alpha parses its colour on every call (low)

`src/js/explosions.js:388`: a regular expression, three `slice` and three `parseInt` per call, called for every gradient stop of the glows, trails and teleports each frame. 54 KB a frame in arcade-w6-z50 and 36 KB in fight-w4-z50. Patch 0001 keeps each colour's `rgba(r, g, b, ` in a map bounded to 512 colours; the strings returned are the same, checked on 56 colour and alpha pairs (case, 8-digit hex, short hex, names, `-0`, long fractions). After: 12 and 7 KB.

### WE-19 sprite lookups build a key string per call (low)

`src/js/sprites.js:23` built `3d:<name>` on every call, `src/js/sprites.js:96` joined colour, size and part on every call. `sprite_raster` 30 KB a frame in arcade-w6-z50. Patch 0002 keys the drawings by their name as it is (a plain name has no slash, a path has one, so they cannot collide) and the rasters by colour, part and size in nested maps. After: `sprite_raster` 13 KB; `sprite` itself 66 → 58 KB, the rest coming from callers that pass names built per call (`worlds/${slug}/…`), which a lookup has to flatten.

### WE-20 six lists are filtered twice a frame (low)

`src/js/update.js:76` and `src/js/update.js:523` filter `particles`, `rings`, `labels`, `trail`, `bullets`, `hostile`, `enemies` and `pickups` into new arrays, and `src/js/map.js:263`–`268` filters `enemies`, `bullets`, `hostile`, `pickups`, `particles` and `trail` again in the same frame (`update_gravity_objects`, called from `src/js/update.js:547`). Each `filter` copies the list. Not fixed: removing one pass changes when a dead object leaves a list within the frame, so it needs its own check of every caller; compacting in place would change the arrays other code may still hold.

### WE-21 fights run 1 to 4 major collections every 5 seconds (medium)

`data/perf/base1`: fights and the arcade show 1–4 major collections per 300 frames (`gc.major`), calm flight 0–2. The longest collection in a fight read 20–67 ms under tonight's load; the 5 October report measured the major one at 20 ms without load. Particles, rings, labels, shots and explosions live about a second, long enough to be promoted, so they die in the old generation. The fix is a pool per kind (reuse the objects instead of making new ones), which touches every place that makes and drops them; not done tonight.

### WE-22 under the measuring harness the arcade's heap grows on the cleared screen (info)

`bin/perf`'s arcade scene keeps firing the pulse and EMP every 2–3 s while the cleared screen holds the simulation, so the waves it makes are never aged out: 5.3 → 6.5 MB from 60 s to 240 s, all of it `pulse_wave_create` (`src/js/pulse.js:91`) under `window.__perf.drive`. A player cannot do this: `pulse()` returns unless `state === 'playing'` (`src/js/combat.js:360`). In flight, no growth: fight-w0-z100 5.4 → 5.8 MB and calm-w6-z50 4.9 → 5.4 MB after collection over 4 minutes, DOM nodes flat (3 181–3 182, 3 016).

### WE-23 a resize freezes the page for about 0.6 s in software (low)

Going from 1366 × 768 to 2560 × 1440 at ×2: three long tasks of 200–280 ms, in three rounds out of three. Its CPU profile: `resize()` 28 ms of script, `setTransform` 94 ms, and 1.4 s of native work (the canvas, 4352 × 2448 at the game's 1.7 cap, reallocated and rastered on the CPU). The script's share is small; on a graphics card the raster leaves the main thread. Worth a check on a real laptop before any change.

### WE-24 the 3D dev mode doubles the script time of a frame (info)

`build/dev.html?world=0&screen=combat`, the readout's milliseconds (the script inside a frame, `src/js/dev/fps.js:10`), median of 20 samples, two rounds: 2D 4.2 and 5.5 ms, 3D 10.9 and 10.2 ms, 3D with `shadows=0` 6.9 and 8.8 ms. In SwiftShader both draw 2–5 frames a second, so the frame rate here says nothing about a laptop with a graphics card; the readout there will.

## What is good

- No leak: the heap, the DOM and the listeners are flat over 4 minutes of a fight.
- The fixes of 5 October hold: the sky layer, the ring glows, the collisions and the HUD writes are nowhere near the top of the profiles.
- The script is small beside the raster: in the CPU profiles `(program)` (native drawing and the collector) takes 88–93% of the time spent, idle left out; the largest functions of the game are `render_ore_scanner`, `update` and `missile_trail_draw` at under 1 ms a frame each.
- `bin/perf` replays a scene frame for frame, so the picture before and after a change compares exactly: all six scenes compared after the fixes are identical to the pixel, and their HUD text to the letter.

## Recommended order of work

1. Apply patches 0001–0004 (WE-16 to WE-19): independent of each other, each one file or two.
2. Pools for particles, rings, labels and shots (WE-21), then measure the major collections again.
3. One filter pass per list a frame (WE-20), with its own check of every caller.
4. On the author's laptop: a fight at 50% in Eclipse and a resize to fullscreen in Chrome's Performance panel (WE-23), and `dev.html?render=3d` against `dev.html` by the readout (WE-24).
