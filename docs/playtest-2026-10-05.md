# World Explorer: arcade playtest, 5 October 2026

How engaging and how meaningful the arcade is at each difficulty, measured by
bots that play it through the keyboard, with the numbers behind each claim.
A short look at the campaign start is at the end. Build: commit 2031ef4.

## How it was played

A bot drove `build/dev.html` in Chromium (Playwright) through real key
presses: WASD to steer (eight directions, as a person on a keyboard), Shift
past missiles, Space for the pulse, E for the EMP, F for stasis, Q for a
repair kit. Guns fire by themselves. At the depot the bot clicks the Buy
buttons. Game time is the game's own `run_time`, not the wall clock.

| Bot | How it flies | How it buys |
|---|---|---|
| decent | holds 320 m from raiders and circles them; sees two shots in three, 0.2 s late, and steps aside; picks up salvage when fewer than three raiders are close; pulse at three raiders within 380 m, EMP at six within 550 m or when hurt, stasis at eight | repair below 75% hull, keep two repair kits, then modules by value per price (Prism, Hotter plasma, Rapid resonance, Phase armor, Shield capacitor, drones, nanites, pulse modules); with the modules bought, a sturdier ship; EMPs up to three |
| casual | holds 250 m; sees two shots in five, 0.3 s late; pulse at four raiders; EMP only below 35% hull; no stasis | as decent |
| expert | holds 75% of gun reach, sees every shot at once | as decent |
| greedy | dives at the nearest raider and every pickup, pulse whenever a raider is within 400 m | damage modules only, repairs below 30% |
| passive | sits still, never presses anything | nothing |

27 runs: decent with three seeds at each difficulty, the others with one or
two, and decent once with each other gun. 18 more runs on the same build
repeated the decent and casual ones with other seeds, the guns bought
without saving up, and each raider shot tagged with who fired it; the
verdict counts both sets, the tables below are the first set. A seed does
not replay a run exactly (the menu's animations draw from the same dice), so
two runs of one seed differ. A death restarts the world from its first
wave, as loading an arcade save does; three deaths in one world end the
run. The bot scripts, every run's record and frames every 3 s of one run
are in `data/playtest/` (not in git).

## Verdict

| Difficulty | Engaging | Meaningful |
|---|---|---|
| CHILL | For one world. Haven costs the decent bot 23 hull and shield over the world; from Verdant on, 2 to 44 a world, and no hull at all. | No. Every bot won, none died, and it ends with 2500 to 6500 salvage it could not use. |
| NORMAL | For two worlds and the last flagship. Haven's flagship wave is the hardest moment of the run: the casual bot lost 120 to 230 hull there. From Dustfall to Nova Forge a wave costs the decent bot 0 hull. | Barely. The depot matters for two visits; after that it is "buy a bigger ship, buy EMPs". Decent won 7 of 7 runs, casual 5 of 6; the one loss died three times in Verdant. |
| OVERLOAD | Yes, unevenly. Haven and Verdant hurt (180 to 300 lost a world), the middle relaxes, and Eclipse's flagship is a wall. | More than the others, but the wall decides it: the decent bot won 2 of 6 runs, the casual bot 0 of 3, and most of their deaths are in Eclipse. |

A whole run takes 6 to 8 minutes of game time: a world 40 to 90 s, a wave 10
to 25 s. The pace is dense. Stretches with no raider in view longer than 4 s
add up to 0 to 22 s a world.

## What works

- **The pace.** Squadrons arrive every 3 to 4 s, a world is over in under a
  minute and a half, and the bot found almost no dead time.
- **Every flagship wave is an event.** The ring, the escorts at 66% and the
  last stand at 33% make wave 3 the busiest part of every world (14 to 24
  raiders alive at the peak).
- **Movement is the skill, as it should be.** On NORMAL the passive bot dies
  in Haven in 11 to 15 s, the casual one takes 213 there, the decent one 144,
  the expert 49.
- **Each world hurts in its own way.** The sources follow the world's gun:
  scatter in Verdant, seekers in Dustfall, ion in Ion Reach, rails in
  Obsidian and Eclipse, torpedoes in Nova Forge.
- **The abilities pull their weight early.** The pulse and the EMP make 64%
  of Haven's kills on NORMAL and 41% of Verdant's.

## Problems, ranked

1. **The difficulty runs backwards.** The worst of the run is Haven's
   flagship wave (NORMAL casual: 173 of its 213), where the chasers' guns
   deal 47% of Haven's damage and the flagship's ring 27%. From Dustfall to
   Nova Forge the decent bot loses 0 hull a wave on NORMAL and CHILL. Your
   offence grows about forty times (cannon 74 damage a second at the start,
   about 3000 with Prism 2, Hotter plasma 5, Rapid resonance 3 and tier 5);
   a chaser's hull grows four times (44 to 191) and its armour and shield
   only to 33% and 100, because the arcade halves them (`arcade_defense`).
   Late raiders die before they reach the screen.
2. **OVERLOAD ends in a wall of rails.** On OVERLOAD the decent bot lost 0
   to 51 hull a wave from Dustfall to Nova Forge, then died in Eclipse three
   times in two of three seeds; the third got through with 504 hull lost in
   the flagship wave and nine repair kits. Eclipse cost it 730 to 2200, and
   Eclipse holds 9 of the 12 deaths the decent and casual bots had on
   OVERLOAD. The killer is not the flagship: every raider there fires a rail,
   63 a hit (30 × 1.75 for the mode × 1.2 for OVERLOAD), at 1250 m/s, tanks
   every 1.1 s and the rest every 1.4 s. Of Eclipse's damage, tanks dealt
   29%, shooters 23%, lancers 19%, the flagship's aimed shot 14%, its ring
   2%. A rail crosses 400 m in a third of a second, so it cannot be dodged
   the way the other guns can.
3. **Upgrades stop mattering after the second depot.** The offence costs 758
   salvage and is bought out by then; the third and fourth depots fill the
   rest of the useful modules. From the fifth on the only choices left are a
   bigger ship and more EMPs.
4. **Salvage is tight for two depots, then loose.** 470 to 590 at the first
   two, 1000 to 1450 from Obsidian on; 1500 to 3100 is left at the end (6500
   for the greedy bot). At 10 points each it is worth 15k to 30k of a 650k
   score, so keeping it is not a real choice either.
5. **Guns are never right.** No gun beat the Pulse cannon with Prism; the
   railgun and the launcher made runs 30% longer, and saving up for the
   railgun or the beam was fatal in Verdant.
6. **Reading the fight.** Raiders come in at 640 m and are fought at 300 to
   430 m, which is the edge of a 1600 × 900 window; squadrons from the north
   pass under the score and the RAIDERS banner, and the flagship bar covers
   them (frames in `data/playtest/`). From Obsidian on most kills happen off
   the screen. This is layout and zoom, not numbers; noted, not changed.
7. **The EMP is cheap.** 26 salvage for 160 + 12 × threat to every raider
   within 650 m: it clears a Haven or Verdant squadron outright (15 to 27
   kills a world on NORMAL). Not changed: once the offence is bought the
   bot has salvage to spare anyway, and problem 1 is the bigger one.

## Decisions for the author

- **Should the shop cost more, or salvage come slower?** Problems 3 and 4
  are one question: how many depots should still offer a hard choice? A
  price factor for modules in the arcade (as `arcade_ship_price` is for
  ships) or fewer artifacts from rocks would stretch the offence over four
  or five depots. It changes how the run feels, so it is yours.
- **What is a gun for in the arcade?** Today a gun is a side grade at best.
  Making one right for a world (rails against Obsidian's armour, ion against
  Cryosphere's shields) needs raiders with more armour and shield than the arcade's
  half share (`arcade_defense`) gives them, or numbers of the gun's own.
- **What should a rail be in the arcade?** It is the one raider gun you
  cannot dodge, and on OVERLOAD the mode makes it fire every 1.1 to 1.4 s
  for 63. Slower rails (dodgeable), rarer rails, or lighter rails each fix
  Eclipse differently; which one keeps the rail's character is yours.
- **The flagship's ring count.** 18 shots a volley in every world but
  Dustfall, where it is 10: `(wave === 5) ? 10 : (wave === 10) ? 14 : 18` in
  `src/js/update.js` keys on the campaign's old sector numbers, and Dustfall's
  threat is 5. A ring that grows with the world is a design choice; the
  ring deals 27% of Haven's damage and 2% of Eclipse's, so it is not urgent.
- **Unspent salvage at the end.** 10 points each is too little to make
  saving a strategy. Worth more, or nothing?

## The campaign start

- A new campaign starts docked at Haven Anchorage with 90 salvage and four
  contracts on the board (story, lure, hunt, mining). The NEXT strip says
  what to do: take a contract, or open the map.
- With "Clear the patrol lanes" taken and the guide flying (T), the bot only
  pressed T. In 3 minutes the guns killed 23 raiders, 1 or 2 at a time, and
  the ship lost 22 hull. The second stage (destroy 10) took about 100 s of
  waiting at its marker.
- It is safe and slow, the opposite of the arcade. Salvage went from 90 to
  123 before the contract's 360.
